# ACI Definition

#module #architecture

> **Artificial Civilization Intelligence (ACI)** is a proposed class of intelligent system architecture designed to maintain a persistent model of civilization and its environment, coordinate heterogeneous intelligent agents and humans, reason across interconnected domains, conduct scientific and technological discovery, plan over long horizons, interact with real-world infrastructure, learn from outcomes, and operate under explicit governance and authority constraints.

## Key Properties

1. **Persistent World Model** - Continuously updated representation of civilization ([[OMNIS World Model]])
2. **Multi-Agent Coordination** - Heterogeneous agent collaboration ([[NEXUS Agent Fabric]])
3. **Scientific Discovery** - Automated hypothesis generation and testing ([[FORGE Scientific Engine]])
4. **Long-Horizon Planning** - Days to decades planning with replanning ([[ASCEND Planner]])
5. **Governed Interaction** - Explicit authority constraints on real-world action ([[VEIL Security]])
6. **Continuous Learning** - Outcomes feed back into world model
7. **Human-in-the-Loop** - Critical decisions require human approval

## What ACI Is NOT

- ACI is **NOT** AGI (Artificial General Intelligence)
- ACI is **NOT** ASI (Artificial Superintelligence)
- ACI is a **PROJECT-DEFINED RESEARCH CONCEPT**
- It is not an established scientific classification
- The purpose is to provide a **testable framework** for the [[ORION]] architecture

## Benchmark

The [[ACI Definition|ACI-001 Benchmark]] evaluates systems against:
- World model fidelity
- Multi-agent coordination quality
- Scientific discovery validity
- Planning horizon and accuracy
- Governance compliance
- Real-world interaction safety
